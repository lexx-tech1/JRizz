The Idea, in Four Scenes

Scene 1 · Before Rizal

A scattered colony

More than 7,000 islands and over a hundred languages. People called themselves Tagalog, Ilokano or Cebuano. In Rizal's time, “Filipino” usually meant a Spaniard born in the islands, not the people who lived there.

Scene 2 · The pen

Words instead of weapons

Rizal chose the pen over the sword. From Europe he wrote novels, essays and letters that named the abuses of colonial rule, in Spanish, so that rulers and the educated could not look away.

Scene 3 · The word spreads

A shared story

Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo were banned, smuggled and passed hand to hand. Readers on different islands saw their own towns in the same pages, and began to see themselves as one people with the same wrongs.

Scene 4 · A nation

A sun and three stars

His generation claimed the name “Filipino” for everyone born in the islands. On 12 June 1898, less than two years after his execution, independence was declared under a flag whose sun has eight rays, one for each of the first provinces to revolt.
